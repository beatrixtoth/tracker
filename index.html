Understanding the run types▸
Easy×1 pts/km
Aerobic base + recovery
The bulk of your mileage. Run at a relaxed, conversational pace — you should be able to talk in full sentences. It builds your aerobic engine and helps your legs absorb harder sessions without piling on fatigue.
Long run×1.3 pts/km
Endurance + race-day toughness
Your longest run of the week, run slower than easy pace. It's less about speed and more about time on your feet — teaching your body (and head) to keep going when you're tired, which is exactly what race day demands.
Tempo×1.6 pts/km
Raises your threshold
A sustained "comfortably hard" effort — you could only manage a few words at a time. It trains your body to clear lactate faster, so paces that once felt hard start to feel sustainable.
Speed×1.8 pts/km
Top-end speed + efficiency
Short, fast repeats (around 5k effort) with easy jog or walk recovery in between. Push genuinely hard on each rep, then let the recovery be properly easy so you can hit the next one well.
Shakeout×1 pts/km
Keeps legs loose, adds nothing
A very short, easy jog — usually in race week. The goal isn't fitness, it's just keeping your legs moving and blood flowing without adding any real fatigue before race day.
Race day×3 pts/km
The one it's all for
Run it at the pace you've been practising in your tempo and race-pace sessions. Stick to the fuelling and hydration you rehearsed on your long runs — race day is not the day to try something new.
